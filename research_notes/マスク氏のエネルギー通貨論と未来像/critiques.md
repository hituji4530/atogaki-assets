## Q1. Economists' responses to "money becomes irrelevant" and "universal high income"

### Takeaway
The main economist critique is that abundance in manufactured goods moves scarcity elsewhere rather than ending it: to land and location, status and positional goods, and human attention and time. Money stays useful as the way to ration those. A second line of critique asks who owns the AI and robot capital, and who pays for UHI and how. Academic models (Korinek, Acemoglu) back caution on both the speed of change and how its gains would be distributed. I found no direct responses to Musk's 2036 claim from Acemoglu, Krugman, Summers, Brynjolfsson, the IMF or the BIS.

### Cited Findings
**Scarcity relocates (positional goods, land, attention)**
- Tyler Cowen (George Mason University) and Noah Smith (economics writer, Noahpinion) pushed back directly. Their argument, as reported: cheaper manufactured goods will not end scarcity, which will shift to status goods, desirable locations, and access to the attention of sought-after people, and money will still ration access to these. — [Yahoo Finance / 24/7 Wall St.](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/elon-musk-predicts-money-won-192156116.html); [Forbes (Birnbaum)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidbirnbaum/2026/08/13/elon-musk-says-money-wont-matter-by-2036-scarcity-has-other-plans/). (The original Cowen and Smith texts were not retrieved; the wording is secondhand.)
- Forbes contributor David Birnbaum, in a piece headlined "Elon Musk Says Money Won't Matter By 2036. Scarcity Has Other Plans", argued that ownership of productive AI and robots will still require a distribution system. — [Forbes, 2026-08-13](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidbirnbaum/2026/08/13/elon-musk-says-money-wont-matter-by-2036-scarcity-has-other-plans/)
- Japanese coverage cites US research institutions and others: robots can mass-produce industrial goods, but things that cannot be multiplied stay scarce even as society grows richer, such as land with a particular view, places at elite schools, and popular people's time. — [hashout.jp](https://hashout.jp/ai/4539/)
- The same Japanese coverage invokes William Baumol's "cost disease": goods machines can make get much cheaper, while the prices of labor-intensive services rise, so value shifts toward scarce human labor. — [hashout.jp](https://hashout.jp/ai/4539/)
- The same coverage reports a Cowen and Smith comparative-advantage argument that humans could keep earning wages. It also cites a view that the real constraints are energy supply, land for data centers and robot factories, and the pace at which institutions can absorb labor displacement. — [hashout.jp](https://hashout.jp/ai/4539/)
- A search summary reported that Adrian Wooldridge (Bloomberg Opinion) argued Musk overlooks the role of status and relative position. I could not confirm that this column exists; see Gaps.
- ITmedia Business Online (2026-07-30) ran a piece examining Musk's "2036, money will no longer be needed" claim against history ("人間が労働から解放される説を歴史から考える"). — [ITmedia via d menu news](https://topics.smt.docomo.ne.jp/article/itmedia_business/trend/itmedia_business-20260730_022)

**Politics, distribution and ownership**

Fortune reports that his essay warns AI could produce a world where a small elite thrives while everyone else faces economic instability. - Analysts argue that if a few firms control the robots and the energy that powers them, the gains of abundance would not be distributed automatically. — [Yahoo Finance / 24/7 Wall St.](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/elon-musk-predicts-money-won-192156116.html); [CEOWORLD](https://ceoworld.biz/2026/08/27/elon-musk-declares-money-will-be-irrelevant-is-he-right/)
- Anton Korinek (University of Virginia) and Donghyun Suh, "Scenarios for the Transition to AGI" (NBER w32255, 2024): wages depend on a race between automation and capital accumulation. If task complexity is bounded and full automation arrives, wages collapse. Wages can fall even before then if automation outpaces capital accumulation. In the aggressive-AGI scenario, returns concentrate quickly with capital owners. — [NBER w32255](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32255/w32255.pdf); [arXiv 2403.12107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12107v1)

- NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani (PBS, September 2026) said "talk is cheap" and that Musk should back his support for living standards with action. — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/mamdanis-challenge-musk-universal-high-income-proposal-1822411)
- Bill Gates (2017 Reddit AMA): "Even the U.S. isn't rich enough to allow people not to work." This is old and not a response to Musk. — [AOL Finance](https://www.aol.com/finance/universal-high-income-elon-musk-180056958.html)
- A Substack essay, "Both Sides Are Wrong About Universal High Income", argues that 50 years of productivity gains already "ran that test": extra output did not automatically flow to workers. — [The Cost of Work (Substack)](https://thecostofwork.substack.com/p/both-sides-are-wrong-about-universal) (non-expert opinion)

**Speed of AI productivity gains**
- Daron Acemoglu (MIT, 2024 Nobel laureate), "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (NBER w32487; published in Economic Policy, 2025): AI raises total factor productivity (TFP) by no more than 0.66% over 10 years, possibly under 0.53%, because the remaining gains are in hard-to-learn tasks. He contrasts this with Goldman Sachs' forecast of a 7% rise in global GDP. This is not a direct reply to Musk, but it is the most-cited mainstream counterweight to near-term abundance claims. — [NBER w32487](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/166327)
- Counter-critique: a LessWrong post argues that Acemoglu's inputs (exposure estimates and GPT-3.5/4-era experiments) are unlikely to hold for 10 years. — [LessWrong, "Contra Acemoglu on AI"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/viRn7Drv9FKcdFpyX/contra-acemoglu-on-ai)

### Inferences
- The economist consensus, as far as it was found, is "abundance yes, end of money no". Even sympathetic voices (Khosla, and Saylor below) accept cheaper goods but reject the claim that money stops mattering.
- Musk's deflation argument and his "issue dollars to people" argument together amount to a monetary-policy claim, not the end of money: a UHI scheme still needs a currency, a fiscal authority and a distribution rule. That point is implied by the Fox Business and Forbes critiques, but no source states it in those exact words.

### Gaps
- I found no direct comments on Musk's 2036 claim from Acemoglu, Krugman, Summers, Brynjolfsson, the IMF or the BIS. They may exist in venues not indexed (FT, NYT, WSJ, Project Syndicate). Searches returned none.
- I could not confirm that Adrian Wooldridge's Bloomberg Opinion column on the topic exists.
- Japanese economists: no named Japanese economist's critique was found in Nikkei or Toyo Keizai. Japanese coverage was mainly ITmedia, hashout.jp, CoinPost and note posts.
- Original Cowen and Smith texts were not retrieved, so their exact quotes are unverified.

## Q2. Monetary-theory critiques of energy-backed currency

### Takeaway
Energy fails several classic tests of money. Its price varies widely by time and place (so it is a poor unit of account), it degrades in storage (so it is a poor store of value), and it is not uniform across time and location (so it is not fungible). The historical precedent, the Technocracy movement's 1930s "energy certificates", deliberately could not be saved or traded and never got beyond proposal. Central banks (BIS) have long argued that the claimed "energy backing" of proof-of-work crypto does not make it money. Rigorous mainstream-economist critiques aimed specifically at Musk's energy-currency remark were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Technocracy Inc. (Howard Scott, 1930s) proposed replacing money with energy certificates denominated in ergs or joules. By design they could not be saved, lent, traded between individuals, or earn interest. — [Tandfonline: "A non-conforming technocratic dream: Howard Scott's technocracy movement" (2024)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17449359.2024.2343657); [MIT DSpace working paper: "From Technocracy to Net Energy Analysis"](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/2023/SWP-1353-09057784.pdf)
- The 2024 academic article argues that Scott's preoccupation with efficiency kept him from questioning economic growth itself, even though his own analysis implied energy scarcity would hinder development. — [Tandfonline (2024)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17449359.2024.2343657)
- Free-market critique: the problem goes beyond Hayek's information problem facing central planners, because energy planners lack private-sector stakes in investment decisions. — [Master Resource, "The Perils of Energy Technocracy"](https://www.masterresource.org/energy-planning-technocracy/the-perils-of-energy-technocracy/) (advocacy outlet)
- Unit-of-account problem: a joule is a fixed physical quantity, but its economic value is not. A megawatt-hour is worth roughly $20 wholesale versus $160 retail, and spot prices can multiply in a cold snap. — [John Hunt MD, "Can Energy Become Money?" (Crisis Investing)](https://www.crisisinvesting.com/p/can-energy-become-money) (investment newsletter, not academic)
- Store-of-value problem: stored energy degrades. "Batteries self-discharge, pressurized hydrogen seeps through steel, water pumped uphill evaporates." — [Crisis Investing](https://www.crisisinvesting.com/p/can-energy-become-money)
- Dimensional critique: power (joules per second) and energy have different dimensions, so energy flows cannot simply be denominated in money units. — [Resilience.org, "The Joule Standard" (2013)](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2013-02-06/the-joule-standard/)
- Integrity risk: if energy-certificate issuance exceeds real energy availability or redemption capacity, the system fails, much as gold-standard constraints did. — [Discover Thought, "Energy as the Ultimate Global Currency: A Critical Analysis"](https://www.discoverthought.org/post/energy-as-the-ultimate-global-currency-a-critical-analysis) (blog quality)
- Fungibility research: units of Bitcoin carry traceable histories and are therefore not perfectly fungible. — [arXiv 2111.04483, "Revisiting the Properties of Money"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.04483)
- BIS Annual Economic Report 2018 (crypto chapter): cryptocurrencies are "speculative assets rather than money" and a "poor substitute for the solid institutional backing of money". Stablecoins are "only as good as the governance behind the promise of the backing." — [Asia Times coverage](https://asiatimes.com/2018/06/central-banks-say-cryptos-no-substitute-for-existing-system/); [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2018/06/18/180741.htm)
- BIS (2021–2022): Bitcoin has "few redeeming public interest attributes", and structural flaws make crypto unsuitable as the basis of a monetary system. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/74403/bitcoin-has-few-redeeming-public-interest-attributes-bis); [Forkast](https://forkast.news/warnings-crypto-bitcoin-materialized-central-bank-bis/)
- Peter Schiff (gold advocate) dismissed Bitcoin as a fake asset in response to Musk's energy remarks. — [Forbes Digital Assets](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2025/12/21/the-true-currency-750-billion-tesla-ceo-elon-musk-sparks-wild-bitcoin-speculation-amid-infinite-money-glitch-debate/)
- Live Trading News says mainstream financial media largely dismissed the idea as tech-founder hyperbole; the piece itself argues it deserves serious attention. — [Live Trading News, "The Electron Standard"](https://www.livetradingnews.com/the-electron-standard)
- Supportive (non-expert): advocates of an "Energy Based Economy" propose the kWh as unit of account because it cannot be counterfeited or created by a central bank. — [Substack primer (wbalthrop)](https://wbalthrop.substack.com/p/why-the-ebe-treats-energy-not-currency)

### Inferences
- Musk's own premise works against his conclusion. If abundant cheap energy (solar, space solar) is the goal, an abundant energy unit would lose value fast, which is the opposite of the scarcity that gives a unit monetary value. His "energy can't be legislated" argument rests on scarcity; his abundance future removes it. (This is my analytical inference. No cited source frames it exactly this way.)
- Energy as a measure of real cost, or an anchor for the economy, is different from energy as a medium of exchange. The first is defensible, as in net-energy analysis (MIT working paper lineage); the second faces the unit-of-account, storage and fungibility problems above.

### Gaps
- No BIS, IMF or central-bank document specifically addresses "energy as currency" as Musk framed it.
- Eric Budish's "The Economic Limits of Bitcoin and the Blockchain" (NBER, 2018), a leading academic critique of proof-of-work security costs, was not surfaced in searches. Its findings are not cited here.
- Most energy-money critiques found are from blogs or newsletters, not peer-reviewed monetary economics. The report should state that the academic literature responding to this specific claim is thin.

## Q3. Physical feasibility: energy and compute constraints, space data centers, and Musk's track record

### Takeaway
Experts (Breakthrough Institute, Brookings, MIT Technology Review, WEF, Scientific American, CNBC sources) see space data centers as possibly viable in the 2030s at best. The binding constraints are cooling (radiators only), radiation hardening and redundancy, chip obsolescence, and launch costs, which need to fall from about $1,500/kg to about $200–250/kg. Some analysts (Forethought) are more optimistic about cooling. Musk's record on timelines for Optimus, FSD and robotaxis is one of consistent slippage, which weakens a 2036 deadline.

### Cited Findings
**Space data centers**
- CNBC (2026-09-07): Musk targets a first launch in late 2027, but experts say significant scale is more likely in the 2030s. Four obstacles are listed. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/07/data-centers-space-elon-musk-obstacles.html)
- WEF (June 2026): cooling is the real obstacle. A two-sided radiator at about 20°C emits only about 633 W/m², based on Starcloud's own white paper. — [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2026/06/space-data-centres-cooling/)
- Brookings: some scientists estimate one orbital facility would need about 2.15 million sq ft of radiators. Brookings frames the "feasibility gap" as a governance risk. — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/orbital-data-centers-feasibility-gap-is-a-governance-risk/)
- Breakthrough Institute: radiation protection may require triple-redundant systems, multiplying launch mass and cost. Google researchers concede in-orbit repair is impractical. — [Breakthrough Institute, "Data Centers Won't Be In Space Anytime Soon"](https://thebreakthrough.org/issues/energy/data-centers-wont-be-in-space-anytime-soon)
- Forethought: space solar becomes competitive at about $250/kg to orbit, against roughly $1,500/kg today. Forethought finds radiative cooling "surprisingly manageable", a dissenting optimistic view. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/will-we-really-put-data-centers-in-space)
- Google's projections assume launch below $200/kg by the mid-2030s. An independent model by Turyshev finds that matching ground costs leaves only $250–1,000/kg for launch and spacecraft combined. One expert warns a launched facility could be obsolete within a couple of years. — [Search summary of CNBC, MIT Technology Review and Scientific American coverage](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/03/1135073/four-things-wed-need-to-put-data-centers-in-space/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/data-centers-in-space/)
- Washington Times (2026-10-15): "The case for putting data centers in orbit, and why it probably does not work yet." — [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/oct/15/case-putting-data-centers-orbit-probably-not-work-yet/)
- On the 2036 timeline generally, critics cite global power-grid limits, semiconductor supply chains, raw-material scarcity and geopolitical friction as bottlenecks to a complete economic transformation within ten years. — [Yahoo Finance / 24/7 Wall St.](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/elon-musk-predicts-money-won-192156116.html); [The Hindu Press](https://thehindupress.com/money-will-be-irrelevant-by-2036-elon-musk-predicts-ai-post-scarcity-but-economists-urge-caution/)

**Musk's track record**
- Optimus: Musk predicted about 10,000 Optimus robots in 2025. By January 2026 he acknowledged none were doing useful work at Tesla. The summer-2026 production start slipped to "later this year" in the Q2 2026 update, and Musk called output "literally impossible to predict." — [Electrek, 2026-04-22](https://electrek.co/2026/04/22/tesla-optimus-production-fremont-model-sx-line/); [Electrek, 2026-07-02](https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/musk-shuts-down-optimus-4d-chess-theory/); [The Driven, 2026-04-23](https://thedriven.io/2026/04/23/musk-delays-release-of-roadster-optimus-and-unsupervised-fsd-as-tesla-margins-jump/); [Tesla 10-Q, Q2 FY2026 (primary source; no unit numbers)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026049270/tsla-20260630.htm)
- FSD and robotaxi: one tracker counts 30+ specific FSD claims, nearly all missed, for example about 1 million robotaxis promised for 2020 against zero delivered. Unsupervised FSD by end-2025 did not happen. On the Q1 2026 call, Tesla said robotaxi and Optimus ramps would be "quite slow". — [Tesorb tracker](https://tesorb.com/elon-musk-predictions-tracker/) (blog); [Forbes, Brad Templeton, 2026-04-22](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bradtempleton/2026/04/22/elon-musk-details-fsd-upgrades-slowed-robotaxi-rollout/)

### Inferences
- The thesis depends on a chain of predictions: humanoid robots at scale, cheap energy, compute in orbit, and launch costs below $250/kg. Each link has slipped before or is a 2030s-plus proposition, so a 2036 end to money compounds several optimistic assumptions.

### Gaps
- No expert critique specific to xAI's own orbital compute plans was found, as opposed to space data centers generally.
- Mars timeline misses (Musk earlier projected crewed Mars missions in the mid-2020s) were not re-verified in this round.

## Q5. Supportive and nuanced views

### Takeaway
Support comes mostly from tech leaders and investors, not academic economists. Sam Altman's "intelligence cost converges to energy cost" view is the closest mainstream parallel to Musk's energy framing. Michael Saylor and Vinod Khosla give partial or conditional agreement. Mo Gawdat (ex-Google X) has made similar predictions. Supporters agree that goods get cheaper but split on whether money disappears.

### Cited Findings
- Sam Altman (OpenAI), "The Gentle Singularity" (blog, June 2025): once datacenter production is automated, the cost of intelligence should converge toward the cost of electricity. Intelligence and energy are the fundamental limiters, and both become "wildly abundant" in the 2030s. ### Inferences
- The defensible core of Musk's thesis, shared with Altman, is that the marginal cost of many goods and of cognitive services trends toward their energy cost. The contested parts are (a) the timeline (2036), (b) "money becomes irrelevant" rather than "money buys more", and (c) "energy becomes currency" rather than "energy becomes the key input cost".

### Gaps
- Peter Diamandis ("Abundance") and other "abundance economists" (for example, the US "abundance agenda" of Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson, which concerns housing and energy supply, not post-money) were not surfaced with commentary specific to Musk.
- No Japanese economist or Japanese think-tank expert who supports the thesis was found.
